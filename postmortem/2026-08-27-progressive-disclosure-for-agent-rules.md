# Progressive disclosure for agent rules

## Decision

Keep standing governance, path responsibility, and hard protection in root and nearest-local briefs. Put conditional engineering contracts with one coherent Guide or Domain owner and reach them through action-first Context Pointers.

Treat all pointer matches from root and nearest-local briefs as a union, load each owner once, and stop when a standing rule, owner, or executable fact conflicts. Establish every owner seam before removing its standing source.

Use template assertions for deterministic propagation and representative agent dry runs for routing behavior. Keep historical stash material read-only.

## Why

Always loading every conditional contract dilutes the rules needed on every change, while moving standing protection behind pointers makes discovery mistakes unsafe. The split keeps durable safeguards present and makes specialized contracts reachable only on relevant branches.

Root and local pointers can both match one change. Union prevents either scope from erasing the other, load-once avoids duplicate authority and context, and conflict-stop prevents an agent from choosing whichever statement is easiest to satisfy.

Owner-first ordering prevents an authority vacuum: a rule remains protected by its standing source until its unique destination is complete and routable. It also makes an owner gap a migration blocker instead of a defect discovered after deletion.

Template checks can prove that files and anchors survive generation byte-for-byte, but they cannot prove that an agent follows the right branch. Dry runs test that behavioral seam without pretending model behavior is deterministic executable proof; both layers are necessary and neither substitutes for the other.

The stash records an earlier attempt against another baseline. Read-only inspection preserves its historical value while preventing stale files and assumptions from silently becoming current authority.
